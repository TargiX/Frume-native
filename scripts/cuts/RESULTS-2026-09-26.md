# Cut study: 26 September 2026

This report compares two separate broad-lobed experiments. It does not rank or
replace the six existing Living/Crystal/Amoeba styles. The product priority is
to preserve those complex styles, audit their geometry, and expand their
independent variants and piece counts. See the installed-library workflow in
[README.md](README.md).

## Scope and outcome

Two experimental recipes were evaluated on the same 12 explicit seeds at each
of 25, 49, and 100 pieces. All 72 simulations completed. The final geometry
gate accepted 67 cuts and rejected five. Of the accepted cuts, 35 needed no
repair and 32 passed after bounded local repair.

These are local geometry results. The installed game library was not replaced;
there was no TestFlight upload or physical-device test. The lab now exposes
both recipes as candidates, showing their growth before offline repair.

Rounded needs less repair than Organic within this experimental sample.
Organic produces denser elongated lobes and needs more repair and rejection.
Both remain broad-lobed interpretations; this study does not demonstrate the
tight curls shown in the reference paper or arbitrary animal-shaped borders.

## Validation and repair

- Shared-edge ownership and orientation, complete outer frame, closed simple
  contours, positive areas, outlier areas, intersections and retracing.
- Adaptive Bezier flattening with an error bound of 0.05% of the smaller cell
  side. Narrow-region acceptance adds twice that error to the 6% gap target.
- A 20-degree minimum junction angle.
- Only shared seams move, with fixed endpoints and frame. Each proposal is
  encoded, decoded and audited. Maximum displacement is 6% of a cell plus the
  codec quantization allowance; at most 24 passes are allowed.
- Final decoded payloads pass all four quarter-turn checks and the existing
  game-piece conversion preserves the expected piece count.

The audit is numerical at its stated tolerance. The area sum is a consistency
check, not a polygon-union certificate. The default 6% gap corresponds to
about two pixels on a 330-pixel-wide 100-piece square board; touch usability
and rendering still require review in the game.

The five rejected cases each retain one narrow region, with no reported
intersection, sharp-corner, area, or topology error:

- Organic, 25 pieces: `cut-study-08`, `cut-study-09`.
- Organic, 100 pieces: `cut-study-04`, `cut-study-07`, `cut-study-09`.

They remain visible in the report and have no output in the accepted directory.
No replacement seed was silently generated.

- A paired 9-piece resolution check: `.context/cut-study/refinement-v3/`.

The resolution pair preserves physical piece length, interface width, wave
scales and duration while changing 48 samples per piece to 96. Both cases pass
the final gate, at approximately 2.19 and 29.48 seconds respectively. One pair
does not establish numerical convergence. Initialization, discretization and
projection cadence still warrant a separate convergence study.

The initial validator accepted 59/72. A recorded rejected geometry exposed a
repair-score plateau: improvement stopped at the nominal gap before clearing
the flattening error allowance. A regression test reproduces it. The final
score includes that allowance, and the pass limit was increased from 16 to 24
without relaxing the gap or displacement limits. All original geometries were
rechecked in a separate directory, producing the 67/72 result above.
